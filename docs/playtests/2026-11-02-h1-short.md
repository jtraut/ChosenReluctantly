# H1 (pressure pass) - short playtest, 2026-11-02

**NO BLOCKER.** Smoke check passes. Faster enemies look fair on paper and in stills; I could not hand-play, so fairness is a theory-plus-stills verdict, not a felt one.

- Not observed: any hand play, or any dodging or kiting. This environment has no input-simulation tool for the real run. Anything below about "dodgeable" comes from data and from qa's section 4 evidence, and is labelled as such.

## Fairness of faster enemies
Stills: `docs/playtests/img/h1-pt-run-rogue_0..4.png`, `h1-pt-run-wizard_0..4.png`.

What I saw (observed): an unattended player is swarmed. The Rogue at the 4.5 s still had taken 5 damage with 4 chasers nearby. The Wizard standing still was at 6/26 by 4.5 s with about 6 chasers on top of it. That is a stress picture, not a fairness one; a standing player should lose.

What the numbers say (theory):
- Chaser 5.0 vs walk 6.0: a fleeing player still gains 1 m/s. The gap closes at 5 m/s head-on, so a chaser seen at the edge of the screen (about 8 m) arrives in roughly 1.6 s instead of 2 s. The warning time is about 0.4 s shorter, which is noticeable but not unfair.
- The chaser telegraph is unchanged at 0.45 s (qa confirmed wind-up is not scaled) and its attack range is 1.5 m. So the readable window is the same; what shrinks is the time to reposition before it is on you. Chasers are the only thing that can now arrive while you are busy casting a long card. That is the sort of pressure the director wanted.
- Shooter 3.75 stays well below the walk, and its shot lands about 0.78 s after the wind-up at 7 m (qa). Faster shooters just reposition quicker. Golem 2.5 with a 0.7 s telegraph is still the easiest thing to walk away from.
- Nothing outruns the player, so nothing can catch you from behind unfairly. Packs are the risk: several 5.0 chasers cut off a retreat, which the Wizard still-frame shows well.

Verdict on fairness: fair on the data, with one caution. I did not feel a 5.0 chaser. A real hands-on pass is still needed, preferably the Rogue (melee, stuck in the crowd) running into a wave of 4+ chasers with the dodge on cooldown.

## One note on pace
Cannot be confirmed from stills. By the numbers: enemies reach you about 20% sooner, and qa's sims show the Rogue boss fight getting longer (60.5 s to 66.1 s, because adds swarm faster) while Wizard rooms are essentially unchanged. So rooms should feel slightly busier at the start, but I have no evidence rooms are faster to clear. Worth asking the director whether that matches his hands.

## Top 3 things that work
1. The speed change is almost invisible in the HUD: no new UI, nothing to relearn, telegraph length untouched.
2. Walk 6.0 vs chaser 5.0 keeps "good movement beats chasers" true.
3. The run starts cleanly for both classes with no console noise.

## Problems (ranked, none blocking)
1. **I could not play it.** No input simulation exists for the real run, so I cannot honestly say the 5.0 chaser feels fair. Suggestion for gameplay-dev: a small scripted-input capture (hold a direction, press Space, click) for the run scene, so playtests can show a dodge against a chaser.
2. **Stand-still stills are a stress picture only.** They prove enemies converge fast, not that the fight is fair. See 1.
3. **Wizard no-cards "still wins" (director's own finding) remains.** Speed alone does not hurt a kiting player, as qa's section 4 showed. This belongs to D27, not to me.
4. Wizard HP shows 26 here; H2 is meant to make it 22. Not a problem, noting it.
5. Nothing visibly different about a 5.0 chaser's wind-up cue; I could not tell if the wind-up reads at a glance when five chasers overlap, as in the Wizard still. Worth a look in the hands-on pass.

## Questions for the director
- Does a room feel quicker to you at 1.25, or only more crowded? That decides whether "faster rooms" was met.
- Is the Rogue boss fight getting about 6 s longer acceptable (qa)?
