# Playtest: MB1, the two act-end mini-bosses

**Blocker: NO.**

## Build and setup
- `main` at 394d893 (MB1 qa PASS), Godot 4.7.2, Windows, real renderer, 1152x648 window.
- **Did (observed):**
  - Windowed frame captures and scripted-input captures (`--press`) of `mini_boss_act1` and `mini_boss_act2` as Rogue and as Wizard (a blind, standing player).
  - The capture scenarios `mini_boss_blink`, `mini_boss_dash_combo` and `mini_boss_wet_floor`, timed with `--at-boss-attack`.
  - `bd3a_stacks`.
  - Real run launches at rooms 1, 10, 11 and 21 for the act tint, the act screen and the "Wants Bleed stacks" line.
  - Headless human-bot runs: `run_wave_sim -Run -Seed 40 -Runs 8 -BotPreset human` for Rogue and for Wizard. The Rogue log held 6 finished runs when I copied it (the other two had not printed yet).
- **Could not do:** play by hand. I have no live keyboard or mouse, only scripted taps and single frames. So there is **no feel, no hit-stop, no "oh nice" judgment and no human difficulty read**. Fun is judged from frames and bot data, and labelled as such. The optional hand-played three-act run was not done; the bot runs stand in for flow and are bot data.
- **Not seen in any frame:** the brute's adds (phases 2 and 3) and the duelist's phase 2 (50% HP). A standing player deals no damage, so no boss left phase 1. They are covered only by the bot.
- Frames are in the gitignored `screenshots/pt2/`.

## Smoke check: PASS
- Both scenarios launch with 0 `ERROR`/`WARNING`/`SCRIPT` lines.
- Observed: floor, player, boss and lighting are visible, the camera frames the player, HP reads 30/30 (Wizard 22/22), and the bar reads "Head of Facilities / Act 1 Mini-boss" and "The Corporate Raider / Act 2 Mini-boss". Both show phase notches and an "Armor N% / Resists all elements N%" line.
- Controls: a scripted run in `mini_boss_act1` (`--press=0.5:dodge,0.8:slot_1 ... 2.0:slot_5`) logged all six presses. At 2.3 s the slots Q to R show cooling bars or a spent state, so the card keys respond. I did not watch movement or aim by hand.

## Acceptance line: is each mini-boss clearly different? Yes (from frames)
- **Brute vs normal enemies:** a big orange-yellow box against small red or purple capsules, with a named bar and subtitle. It cannot be mistaken for a chaser.
- **Duelist vs normal enemies:** the same size class as a chaser, but a dark suit with a bright red tie stripe, and it is the only thing in the room with a named bar. It reads as "a person", not "a mob". Its capsule shape is the thing most like a normal enemy; the stripe, the bar and the act-2 room are what sell it.
- **Brute vs duelist:** opposite silhouettes (huge box against a thin capsule) and opposite telegraph styles (wide half-disc and overlapping discs, against a narrow lane and a small wedge).
- **vs the Guardian:** the Guardian is a capsule with a volley. The brute is a box with no ranged attack and multi-circle floors. The duelist has no adds. I did not capture the Guardian in this pass, so this is design plus the shapes I saw, not a side-by-side.
- **The duelist matches the director's example** (small, quick, hits hard, rogue style): 9 damage on a 0.6 s slash is the biggest hit in either fight, and blink-then-backstab is the rogue move. It reads as a duel.

## Top 3 things that work
1. **The blink marker reads as "it lands here".** In `blink_1` (Golden parachute 1/2) the landing spot behind the player is a pale white/cyan ring with a core dot, plus a thin line from the boss. It is the only non-pink telegraph in the game, so it does not read as a damage zone. Then `blink_3` shows "Backstab (2/2)" as the pink wedge, centred on the player. The "(1/2) / (2/2)" cue in the bar's attack line is clear, and the bar's name changes between the two halves.
2. **The dash combo is legible.** "Leveraged dash (1/2)" paints a long lane that fills with pink as the wind-up runs. "(2/2)" re-aims with its own lane (`dash_3`, the lane has turned). Two separate wind-ups with a named step make the combo feel fair, not like a surprise double hit.
3. **The brute's attacks read at a glance.** Mop sweep is a big half-disc outlined in pink with the box inside it; Forklift charge is a long brown lane to the player (the fill is quite dull on the green floor, but the name and the bar carry it); Wet floor is three overlapping pink discs. The bar shows the attack name with a progress line for all of them.

## Top 5 problems, ranked
1. **[should-fix] The duelist is a speed bump for the Rogue, and kills a passive player very fast.**
   - Observed (frames): a standing Rogue was at 12/30 at 5 s and dead by about 9 s. A standing Wizard was at 4/22 at 5 s. The Wizard has no dodge pip to spare (one pip shown).
   - Observed (human bot, 6 Rogue runs): the duelist room took 12 to 16 s, and the bot's lowest HP in it was **30/30 in 5 of 6 runs** (25 in one). The brute room took 24 to 33 s with a lowest HP of 13 to 26. For the Wizard (4 runs that reached it): the brute took 34 to 43 s (lowest HP 9 to 22 of 22) and the duelist 18 to 31 s (lowest 10 to 22).
   - Why it matters to a player: the duelist is the most exciting design on the table, but at about 15 s it ends before its second phase and its blink/backstab loop have been used much. A duel that is over in 15 s does not test reaction timing, and it is meant to end an act.
   - Opinion: I would **raise duelist HP first (90 to about 120), not its damage.** The frames say it already hurts (a passive player dies in 9 s); the bot says it dies before it gets to hurt a player who is moving. More HP means more sequences per fight, which is more of what the duelist is for. If the Wizard then drops under the 9/36 floor, tune damage (as MAP.md §11.4 already says).
2. **[should-fix] The brute is also soft for the Rogue, by HP lost, but it does not feel like a speed bump.** It cost the Rogue bot a median of about 7 HP (30 to roughly 23). It is a real fight (24 to 33 s, adds in phases 2 and 3 by design), just not a threat. For the Wizard it is tougher: lowest HP 9 to 22 of 22. So the brute is "fair for the Wizard, easy for the Rogue", which is the shape the Rogue has everywhere. If you want it harder, raise the **adds** (a third crew member in the last phase) before raising HP: a bigger HP pool would make an already 30 s fight longer without making it riskier.
3. **[should-fix] Wet floor is a "walk out" test, not the "safe pockets" puzzle.**
   - Observed (`wet_1`): the three discs land on and around the player and merge into one blob. There is no safe pocket inside it. The only safe ground is outside the cluster.
   - Why it matters: the move reads fine and is fair (1.1 s of wind-up is enough to walk 4 to 5 m out), but the design talked about reading overlaps for gaps, and the player will just run. It looks heavier than it plays.
   - Suggestion: pin the two outer discs 3.0 m or more from the player so the middle is a real gap, or leave the circles but call it a nit. I recommend the spread change; it is one number.
4. **[nit] Stack labels still collide at 2 enemies.**
   - Observed (`bd3a_stacks`, `stacks_1`): the second chaser's "Bleed x5 MAX" runs into the first chaser's "10" damage number. At the detonate (`stacks_2`) the two "31!" numbers overlap, and so do the two "Bleed detonated" lines. "Bleed detonated" is clear of its **own** number, as intended, but not clear of the neighbour's.
   - **I could not test the 3-plus case.** The scenario has two chasers, and a scripted real fight with `--deck=rogue-bleed-out --relics=serrated_excuse` never produced stacks on three close enemies (one hit landed in `real_2`; no stack label showed). So the ux lift-on-screen rule for 3 or more is unconfirmed. Do not read this as a pass.
5. **[nit] Act 3 floor makes a red chaser a bit weaker.**
   - Observed (`act3.png`, room 21): act 2 (blue-grey) and act 3 (brown-red) are clearly distinct from act 1 (green) and from each other. A red chaser on the act 3 floor is visible, because of the shadow and the darker capsule, but the contrast is clearly lower than on green or blue. It is readable in a calm frame; I did not see it in a crowd.
   - Suggestion: a lighter or more orange chaser highlight on act 3, or a slightly less saturated floor. Not urgent.

Also noted (nits):
- Card names clip at a comma on the slot plates: "Overhand," "Three Bolts," "Personal Space,". They read like typos. The full names are longer ("Overhand, Whatever" is the next-card line); the plate truncates. [nit; seen in several frames]
- Act 1 forklift lane is a dull brown strip on green; the pink part sits only at the end by the boss. [nit]

## The BD3a fixes
- **Stack labels, 3 or more enemies:** not verified (see problem 4). Two enemies: partial overlap.
- **"Bleed detonated" clear of the number:** yes for its own number (a pink line above a white "31!"); the neighbour's number can still overlap.
- **Act screen "Running low" wording:** **not seen.** With the clear at 9 s the screen read "HP 26 of 30, 92 gold, relics: 0", then "You recover 9 HP. Rests are on the map." (white, not red), because 17 plus the 30% heal is above the 50% line. A second capture with the clear at 20 s found the standing player already dead (Game Over, "Reached room 10 of 30"), so a passive player does die to the brute inside 20 s. The low-HP act screen was never reached, so that wording is unverified. Observation worth the director's attention: with a 30% heal and a 50% threshold, "Running low" can show only if the player arrives at 6/30 HP or less. It may almost never appear. The wording that did appear is clear.
- **"Wants Bleed stacks" on Open a Vein:** it shows as a small pill above the slot, in the frames both with Bleed stacks live (`stacks_2`) and in a real run (`real_2`, Open a Vein cooling). It tells you what the card wants, which helps. It does not tell you it is ready now. I would **add a glow when stacks are live on a target in range**, as the Burn cards already get: the pill is read once and then ignored, a glow is read every time. In `real_2` the pill is there with no stacks around, which is correct but shows why a glow would carry more.

## Difficulty verdicts (opinion, from frames and bot data; no human read)
- **Brute (act 1): soft for the Rogue, fair for the Wizard.** It feels like a real fight in length (24 to 43 s) but is a speed bump in risk for the Rogue. Not an act-ending threat. Knob: adds first, then HP.
- **Duelist (act 2): a speed bump for the Rogue (about 15 s, 30/30 HP in 5 of 6 runs), but not soft for a player who stands still.** A moving player barely meets it. Knob: HP 90 to about 120; damage only if the Wizard floor then breaks.
- **Neither mini-boss killed a bot run.** 0 losses at either, in the 6 Rogue runs and the 4 Wizard runs that reached them. The Wizard's 4 early losses (rooms 2 to 6) and 2 late ones (rooms 27 and 29) are not mini-boss deaths. That is one small batch, seeds 40 to 47, human preset.
- A bot with a 0.2 s reaction time is not a new player. A human who has never seen a blink-then-backstab will probably take hits from it. I would expect first-time deaths at the duelist, but I cannot show that.

## Questions for the director
1. Should the duelist be a real threat (HP 90 to about 120) or stay a quick duel? The brief says "act-ending", and today it ends fast.
2. Do you want Wet floor to be a "read the gaps" move (spread the outer discs out) or is "run out of the blob" fine?
3. Should Open a Vein get a glow like the Burn cards, or is the "Wants Bleed stacks" pill enough?
4. Is "Running low" meant to be this rare, or should the threshold or the heal change?
