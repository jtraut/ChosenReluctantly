## 1. Code and tests

- `EnemyConfig.validate_pressure` rejects: non-object, missing key, non-number (string, null, bool), NaN, below 0.25, above 4.0, unknown key. Failure messages all start with `pressure:`. `validate()` rejects a config with the block missing. The loader keeps the validated block (no silent default on bad data; bad data takes the existing `push_error` path).
- `Enemy._apply_stats`: `move_speed *= speed_scale`, `attack_cooldown /= attack_rate_scale`. Wind-up (`telegraph_duration`) is not touched, so the 0.45 s floor holds. Elites are the same scenes with `apply_tier` after `_apply_stats`; `apply_tier` does not touch speed or cooldown, so elites are scaled.
- Boss: `Boss` extends `Enemy`, so its inherited `move_speed`/`attack_cooldown` fields are scaled too, but `Boss.gd` moves with `_phase()["move_speed"]` and gaps with `_phase()["attack_gap"]` only (lines 362, 374, 346, 712). So the boss's real movement and pacing are unscaled. Its summoned adds are normal enemies and are scaled. Non-blocking: the dead scaled fields on the boss are a trap if later code reads `boss.move_speed`.
- Speeds at 1.25: chaser 5.0, shooter 3.75, golem 2.5, all below the player's walk of 6.0. The existing test `test_no_scaled_enemy_outruns_the_player_walk` covers every non-boss id including future ones.
- gameplay-dev's three test edits (`enemy_stats_5d_test`, `slice5d_qa_test`) follow the knob rather than weakening anything: they multiply the expected value by the scale.
- Added by qa: `tests/unit/h1_qa_test.gd` (4 cases, all pass): elites get scaled speed and cooldown with the telegraph unchanged and stay under 6.0; boss adds are scaled (`_summon`) while the boss's own horizontal speed never exceeds its phase 1 data value over 40 physics frames; validation edge cases (0.25, 4.0, int 4 accepted; 0.24, 4.01, negative, NaN, null, bool, string, int, array rejected, each message names "pressure"); the shipped values are pinned at 1.25/1.0 (update this pin deliberately when D27 is answered).

## 4. Why the kiting Wizard takes (almost) no damage

Short answer: **the shooters fire, in range, in numbers; their shots are simply dodged by the bot's ordinary movement.** They are not killed before firing and not out-ranged.

Evidence (a qa-only instrumented copy of the sim, not committed; Wizard kiting, standard policy, 1.25/1.0, seeds 1-8, one full run each):
- Killed before firing? No. 192 shooters spawned over 8 runs; 8 never fired (4%). The rest fired about three times each: 574 shooter telegraphs in total.
- Out-ranged? No. Firing distance clusters at 7.1-7.3 m (their `preferred_range` of 7.0 plus the arrival margin), inside the 18 m projectile range, and the bot's 5 m back-off / 9 m close-in stand-off keeps it in that band. Shooters move at 3.75 and cannot close on a player who walks at 6.0.
- Dodged? Yes, by default movement. Roughly 85 HP of shooter damage over the 8 runs, at 4 per hit about 21 hits from 574 shots: **about 4% of shots hit.** Between 14 and 34 enemy pellets per run (this count includes boss volleys) came within 1.5 m of the player, many at 0.3-0.8 m, and still missed. The mechanism in code: the shooter does not turn during its 0.45 s wind-up (`_physics_process` only counts down), the shot is then fired straight along the facing it had when the wind-up began, with no lead, at 9 m/s with a 0.3 m hit radius. At 7 m it takes about 0.78 s to arrive. A player moving at 6.0 m/s leaves the lane in about 0.1 s, so almost any movement that isn't along the lane dodges it. The bot is always moving (backing off, strafing, closing in) and does not even use `-DodgePellets`.
- Contrast: the same Wizard with `-WizardNoKite` (walks into the crowd) has a median dip of 15.4%, so the damage is there when the player stands in the fight.

Consequences for D27 (my reading; game-lead's call):
- Attack rate cannot fix this: it fires the same dodgeable shots more often (Wizard standard stayed at 95% and 0.6% mean end loss at 1.25/1.25) while it hurts the Rogue badly. If attack rate is the next lever it probably wants to be split (ranged vs melee), which would need a second knob; I have not tested that.
- Levers that act on the cause (all outside H1's two levers, so untested): shooters re-aim just before firing, a faster projectile, a shot fan or double shot, a wider hit lane, or placing shooters so a stand-off puts the player in two lanes at once. Each of these also changes readability (D6), so playtester should look at any of them.
- Speed capped below the player's walk (the brief's rule) is the reason speed alone cannot touch a kiting player.

## 5. What the sim can't tell us

- It's a floor, as the brief says. The bot never plays the way a person does: no cover use, no deliberate baiting, no card timing, no reading of telegraphs except the boss's. A person kiting a shooter may do better or worse than the bot's constant strafing; the director's 5:25 no-cards clear shows humans beat the no-cards policy by a wide margin.
- The no-cards result (0% everywhere) says nothing about H1 because 1.0 already gives 0%. Whether a human now loses before the boss without cards is only answerable by the director playing it.
- Readability and fairness of faster chasers: 5.0 m/s against a 6.0 walk with a 0.45 s telegraph. The sim measures damage, not whether the player saw the hit coming.
- Feel: the sim runs with hit-stop off, a fixed 60 Hz step, and no rendering. Frame-time and input-latency effects on a 25% faster crowd are unmeasured.
- 20 seeds give a standard error of about 11 points on a clear rate near 50%, and about 5 on the 90-95% cells. The 90% vs 95% Wizard difference is not meaningful.
- Elite rooms and the mixed waves are in the runs, but I did not break results out per room type or per enemy type.
